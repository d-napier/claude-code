# AIUC-1 Certification Process

## Overview

- **Timeline**: 4-8 weeks typical (5-10 weeks for most organizations)
- **Validity**: 12 months
- **Technical retesting**: Quarterly (every 3 months minimum)
- **Issuer**: Only the Artificial Intelligence Underwriting Company issues AIUC-1 certificates
- **First accredited auditor**: Schellman
- **Update schedule**: Quarterly — January 15, April 15, July 15, October 15

## Four Phases

### Phase 1: Scoping & Kick-off (1-2 weeks)
- Define product scope
- Assign team members
- Set up environment
- Identify initial evidence and gaps
- Sign contract
- **Result**: Audit & evals scoped, initial gaps identified

### Phase 2: Gather Evidence (3-5 weeks)
- Document operational practices
- Collect legal/governance policies
- Detail technical implementation
- **Result**: Evidence collected, gaps remediated

### Phase 3: Run Technical Testing (1-3 weeks)
- Test for hallucinations
- Test for unsafe tool calls
- Test for adversarial attacks (jailbreaks, prompt injection)
- **Result**: Evals implemented, vulnerabilities mitigated

### Phase 4: Finalize Audit (variable)
- Combine all evidence
- Develop final report
- Obtain signoff
- **Result**: Final audit report delivered, certificate issued

## What You Receive

1. **AIUC-1 Certificate** — Demonstrates trust to enterprise buyers
2. **Comprehensive Audit Report** — Third-party attestation and evaluation results
3. **AIUC-1 Badge** — For websites, footers, or sales materials

## Who Should Pursue Certification

Organizations that:
- Develop agentic AI systems on generative models
- Deploy AI in high-risk scenarios (customer-facing agents, confidential data access, critical workflows)
- Need to demonstrate security to enterprise customers, executives, or boards

Certified agent types include: customer service, candidate scoring, interviewer, automation, image generation, and summarization agents — from seed-stage to publicly traded companies.

## Scope & Limitations

- Focuses on **enterprise risks**: data leaks, IP infringement, jailbreaks
- Less applicable to AI companions or NSFW content generation
- Certificate demonstrates adherence to best practices at certification time — not a guarantee of absolute security
- Technical tests rerun quarterly; full audits occur annually

## How AIUC-1 Differs from Other Standards

| Standard | Difference |
|----------|-----------|
| **ISO 42001** | ISO focuses on AI governance/management systems. AIUC-1 adds independent technical testing validating safeguard robustness. Many pursue AIUC-1 after achieving ISO 42001. |
| **SOC 2** | SOC 2 is the cybersecurity standard but lacks AI specificity. AIUC-1 adopts SOC 2's principles while addressing AI-unique risks. |
| **NIST AI RMF** | AIUC-1 operationalizes NIST's four functions (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage) into concrete, testable controls. |
| **EU AI Act** | AIUC-1 maps risk classifications and makes abstract requirements concrete and auditable. |

## Gap Support

If gap analysis reveals gaps, AIUC has:
- Policy templates
- Technical safeguard implementation blueprints
- Guidance documents
- Limited audit support and advisory services
- Auditor referrals to qualified managed service providers
